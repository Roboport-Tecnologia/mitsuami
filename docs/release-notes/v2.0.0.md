mitsuami 2.0 adds trees, splits, popovers, split buttons, colour wells, search suggestions and rows the user drags to move, lists as high as their rows, each the platform's own, and a C ABI for bindings to other languages.

## What's new

- **`Tree`:** rows that nest, shown by the platform's tree: `NSOutlineView`, a GTK list view of tree expanders, Qt Quick's `TreeView`, and on WinUI a list view whose rows are `TreeViewItem`s. Each draws its own disclosures, indents each level its own way and opens rows with its own keys; the app keeps which rows are open. Rows drag within a tree, onto folders and between rows at the level the pointer picks, and drag their files out. `Tree::loading` shows the platform's spinner in place of a folder's disclosure while its children are read, after half a second, as Finder and Nautilus do.
- **`Split`:** two panes with the platform's divider between them, which the user drags (`NSSplitView`, `gtk::Paned`, Qt Quick's `SplitView`, and on WinUI, which has no splitter, a grip built as File Explorer's pane resizer). Panes have least sizes, the app can move the divider without it being reported as a move, and a hidden pane collapses with its divider.
- **`Popover`:** the platform's popover, shown by a container: an `NSPopover` with its arrow on macOS, a popover on GNOME, a flyout on Windows, a popup on KDE, each insetting its content as the platform does. A click outside or Escape dismisses it, and the app hears it.
- **`SplitButton`:** a button with a menu of other actions beside its own: `NSComboButton` on macOS 13 and later, `adw::SplitButton` on GNOME, WinUI's `SplitButton`, and a button with an arrow beside it on KDE.
- **`ColorWell`:** the platform's colour well, which opens the platform's colour picker. `Color::from_hex` and `Color::hex` go to and from `#rrggbb`.
- **Search suggestions:** a `SearchInput` suggests under the field, as each platform does, while the keyboard stays in the field. `search_on_return` makes a field search only on Return, with each platform's own setting for it, for searches that take a while.
- **A text field's own keys first:** `on_key` on a `TextInput` or `SearchInput` gets the arrows, Return and Escape before the field's editing, for a field that moves through a list beside it, as Xcode's Open Quickly does.
- **Moving rows:** `List::on_move` lets the user drag rows within a list, between rows to reorder them, onto rows (into a group, say), or both (`RowMoves`), and the app moves them in its data. Rows are indented by the platform's tree indentation.
- **The app's own vector icons:** `VectorIcon` takes path data in the SVG syntax (`Icon::vector`, buttons' `vector_icon`), drawn as each platform draws its symbolic icons, so they take the icon colour, dark mode and `color` as named ones do. An app ports its icon set once rather than mapping each icon to four platforms' sets.
- **Lists as high as their rows:** a `List`, `Table` or `Tree` the app doesn't size is as high as the platform lays out its rows, header and border included, and gives way as any node does: it shrinks and scrolls when there isn't room, grows with `grow` and stops at `max_height`.
- **`Length::MaxContent`:** CSS's `max-content`, in grid tracks and for a node's least and most sizes, so two lists in `minmax(auto, max-content)` rows share a sidebar: each as high as its rows while they fit.
- **File icons by name:** `FileLookup` lets a `FileIcon` take the icon of its name's type, or a plain folder's, without reading the file, for files that aren't on disk (an archive's contents, a file only in an old revision). Each platform types the name as its file manager does.
- **`Color::OnAccent`:** the platform's colour for text and icons on an accent fill, a count on a badge, say.
- **`ButtonStyle::Prominent`:** the accent fill without the default button's Return, for a button that stands out among others.
- **Coloured icons:** `icon_color` on a `Button` or `ToggleButton` colours its icon.
- **Field errors:** `error` on a `TextInput`, `PasswordInput` or `TextArea` marks it invalid the platform's way and says why, and assistive technology reads it as invalid.
- **Text areas for logs:** `follows_end` keeps a text area at its end as its text is set, unless the user scrolled up, and a log made with its text opens at its end; `text_style` sets its style (monospace, for one).
- **Menus built as they open:** `menu_on_open` on a `MenuButton`, and `MenuOnOpen` for context menus, build the menu from what's true when it opens: the selection, the moment.
- **Clicks on containers and groups:** `on_click` on a `Container` or `Group`, for a row of the app's own that's clicked as a whole. A click on a control inside stays the control's.
- **Click-through containers:** `click_through` lets clicks pass through a container to what's under it, as a hint laid over a text area. `Color::Placeholder` is the platform's placeholder text colour.
- **Sidebar panes:** `sidebar_pane` marks a pane the app builds that navigates (a rail of pages). On WinUI, under tabs in the title bar, it's on Mica Alt's commanding layer with the chosen tab, as WinUI's guidance has for navigation; on GNOME it takes libadwaita's sidebar style.
- **Cells that fill their row:** `TableColumn::fills_row` sizes a column's cells to their row's height, for a cell with a background of its own.
- **Destructive alert buttons:** `Alert::destructive_button` marks the button that destroys something, which AppKit's alerts draw red.
- **Reviewing visual baselines:** `cargo mitsuami visual review` records each Accept and Reject, with Undo, until Done applies them, and shows what each story's doc comment says to expect on each platform; `cargo mitsuami visual baselines` shows every accepted baseline, each platform's side by side, to reject any. Captures show what users see: AppKit's as the active window, with legacy scroll bars and popovers, GTK's in libadwaita's look, and popovers open on every platform.
- **A C ABI** (`mitsuami-ffi`) over the core's `Ui`, for bindings to other languages: windows, the built-in widgets, lists and tables, trees, menus, dialogs, services, file icons, drawn widgets and captures, and grid tracks and flex-basis in layout. It isn't on crates.io yet: build it from the repository.

## macOS (AppKit)

- **A file dialog AppKit won't show is answered as a cancel, not a crash.** In the App Sandbox, without the `com.apple.security.files.user-selected` entitlement (which a child process that inherits its parent's sandbox can never have), AppKit gives no open or save panel, and the app panicked and closed without a word. Now `open_file` and `save_file` answer `None`, as Cancel does, and mitsuami prints which entitlement is missing.
- **A menu titled with the app's name joins the app menu,** after Settings, where macOS apps put their app-wide commands, instead of standing beside it with the same title.
- **Labels draw where they're placed.** Measured with the text insets AppKit keeps outside their alignment rect, each label was 4 points wider than its text and drew it 2 points to the left: a letter centred in a badge showed at its left.
- **Keys a text view doesn't use reach the views around it,** so a shortcut on a container works while a text area has focus.
- **A bordered button the app makes taller is AppKit's flexible push button,** which draws at that height, rather than a regular one stretched.
- **A split runs up under the title bar,** its scroll view's content under the glass, as Finder's and Mail's do.
- **A popover opened again while it animates closed shows again.**
- **The colour panel closes with its well.**

## Linux (GTK)

- **A header goes in the window's second top bar,** sharing the header bar's background and shade, as libadwaita apps' search and tab bars do, and what's under it starts below everything in the top bars.
- **Sidebar panes take libadwaita's sidebar style.**
- **A popover from a control that shows on hover stays open.** The popover's grab told the row under the pointer it had left, so the control hid and closed its menu as it opened.
- **A split hidden with its page and shown again keeps its divider where it was.**
- **A list shorter than a scroll bar's minimum keeps its height,** scrolling without a bar, instead of spilling over what's below it.
- **A group's heading shows.** Placed before its window showed, a titled group put its card over its heading.
- **A select resizes to the option the user picks,** as it did to the app's: the pick stayed cut off at the old option's width.

## Linux (KDE Plasma)

- **Text areas open at their top,** where a new one showed its text 30 px in, and **unwrapped lines keep their room above the scroll bar,** which Breeze adds once it shows: lines were cut off below it.
- **Rows and tabs drag with a touchpad,** and on Hyprland, whose trackball Qt reports as one, rather than the mouse only.
- **A select resizes to the option the user picks.**

## Windows (WinUI)

- **Selecting several rows works as in File Explorer:** a click selects one, Control and Shift add more, in lists and trees, rather than XAML's check box on every row.
- **Controls with no room no longer draw at their parent's corner.** XAML drew past a zero frame (a check box's least width, a button's padding), so hidden controls showed; now they're clear, out of reach of clicks and out of UI Automation's view.
- **Popovers open under their button** even in a short window, leaving the window as menu flyouts do.
- **Tabs always show a page.** WinUI's selector bar can choose none, which showed an empty page; the core now chooses the first, as it does a select's option.
- **A control centred in a row no longer sits a point low.** The core rounds each frame from its edges' places in the window, as browsers do, rather than adding up each parent's rounding.
- **Rows dropped within a list land where they're aimed.** Every drop had gone to the end.
- **Icon-only buttons keep their icon whole** when they're narrower than their natural width.
- **A select resizes to the option the user picks.**
- **Under tabs in the title bar,** the chosen tab and a header are one surface on Mica Alt's commanding layer, as in File Explorer, and the tabs sit exactly on the title bar's edge.
- **A menu item's shortcut no longer reaches a GPU surface as well as the menu.** A chord that types nothing goes to the surface only if no node's keys or accelerator took it, as on the other platforms, so an editor embedded in an app no longer acts on the app's shortcuts.

## Upgrading from 1.x

- **What mitsuami hands your app to match on is now `#[non_exhaustive]`:** `SurfaceInput`, `KeyCode` and `MouseButton` (a `GpuSurface`'s input) and `ServiceError` (the trash, launching and the clipboard). A `match` on them needs a `_` arm; in return, new kinds of input and errors in later versions won't stop your app compiling.
- **Enums you only pick from gained variants** (`ButtonStyle::Prominent`, `Color::Placeholder`, `Color::OnAccent`, `Length::MaxContent`), as did the backend protocol's (`WidgetKind`, `Prop`, `Event`). Choosing a variant needs no change; code that matches them exhaustively, such as a backend of its own, handles the new ones. These stay exhaustive, and gain variants in minor versions (ARCHITECTURE.md §10, "Enums that grow").
- **A list you didn't size now has a height, its rows'.** A list with a `height`, or stretched or fixed by its parent, keeps its size. In a `ScrollView`, or a window that fits its content, an unsized list is as high as all its rows and the platform makes every one: give it a `height` or `max_height` there.
- **`SelectionMode::Multiple` on WinUI no longer shows check boxes** (XAML's `Extended`, as the other platforms select). An app that wants them sets XAML's `Multiple` in a tweak.
- Nothing was removed, and apps that only build views need no changes.
