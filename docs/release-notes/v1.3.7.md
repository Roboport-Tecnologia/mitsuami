mitsuami 1.3.7 lets a `GpuSurface` show the window's backdrop through what it presents, with macOS's vibrancy under it.

## macOS (AppKit)

- **A transparent `GpuSurface` has the window's vibrancy under it.** `GpuSurface::transparent(true)` puts behind-window vibrancy (an `NSVisualEffectView` with the `underWindowBackground` material, dimmed as the window's background is when it isn't the active one) just under the surface, at its frame, so wherever the app presents with alpha the desktop shows through, blurred. The app configures its surface to composite alpha: wgpu's `PostMultiplied` mode on Metal, which Core Animation reads as premultiplied. A subview can't be under a view's own Metal layer, so the backdrop is a view beside the surface, and children placed after a transparent surface now go above it rather than between it and its backdrop.

## Windows (WinUI)

- **`GpuSurface::transparent` is taken as set.** Every window already has Mica behind the surface's child window, which shows where the app presents with alpha through a composition swapchain (wgpu's `Dx12SwapchainKind::DxgiFromVisual`).

## Linux (GTK and Kirigami)

- **`GpuSurface::transparent` is taken as set.** Neither toolkit has a backdrop material; on Wayland the window's own background shows where the app presents with alpha.
