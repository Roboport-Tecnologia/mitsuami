mitsuami 1.1 lets sidebar items be what a window is about, not only its pages. On Windows, an app can now put its menu bar in the title bar. On macOS, content scrolls under the toolbar as it does in Finder. The macOS apps are now signed and notarized. It's a drop-in update from 1.0.

## Sidebars

Items can now be things the window works on, the way Mail's mailboxes or a VM manager's machines are:

- `SidebarItem::subtitle`: a second line under the title, in the platform's secondary style.
- `SidebarItem::context_menu`: a menu for each item, shown the platform's way (right-click, long press, the menu key).
- `Sidebar::on_activate`: called on a double-click or Enter (WinUI's double-tap).
- `Sidebar::children_with`: items rebuilt whenever what they read changes, for lists that come and go.

On GTK, the primary menu now sits in the sidebar's header bar, where GNOME's guidelines put it, and follows the sidebar's width.

## Windows (WinUI)

- **The menu bar can go in the title bar** after the title, as in Paint and Visual Studio: `winui::set_menu_bar_place(MenuBarPlace::InTitleBar)`, called before `run` in a `platform!` arm. `winui::set_full_screen_menu_bar(FullScreenMenuBar::AtTopEdge)` shows it in full screen while the pointer is at the top edge of the screen.
- **Window placement**: a window opens where Windows puts it, and is kept inside the work area. `winui::set_window_placement(WindowPlacement::Centred)` centres it on its display instead.
- A window keeps its content size when its title bar grows taller.
- The menu bar is rebuilt after a menu closes. XAML's menus otherwise came back wider on their second opening, with checked items' shortcuts out of line.
- `GpuSurface` fixes: a click on the surface closes open menus, the surface stays above XAML's title bar input window, and it hides while an alert is up so the alert isn't drawn under it.

## macOS (AppKit)

- **Content scrolls under the toolbar** beside a sidebar, and a table at the top of a toolbar window runs under the bar too, as in Finder, Mail and SwiftUI apps. macOS 26 blurs what scrolls under it.
- **Windows draw their own background.** On macOS 26 that is no longer a flat colour, so the body now matches the title bar, and a sidebar window matches Finder and System Settings.
- An icon-only button in a toolbar group shows as its image alone.
- While a `GpuSurface` grabs the keyboard, the system's shortcuts are turned off (accessibility shortcuts stay on), so Command-Tab, Command-Space, Mission Control, the Spaces arrows and the screenshot chords reach the surface as plain keys. They come back when the grab ends.

## GTK

- A window being resized is captured at its new size.

## The showcase and Files

Release files no longer have the version in their names, so `releases/latest/download/<file>` links keep working across releases. The macOS apps now come as signed, notarized disk images, and Linux also gets the bare binaries.

- **macOS** (Apple silicon and Intel, macOS 11 or later): disk images, signed and notarized. Open one and drag the app to Applications.
- **Windows** (x86_64, Windows 11): needs the [Windows App Runtime 2.4 or later](https://aka.ms/windowsappsdk/2.5/latest/windowsappruntimeinstall-x64.exe). They aren't packaged, and Windows 10 runs WinUI apps only as packages.
- **Linux** (x86_64): on GTK (`-gtk`) and on Qt Quick and Kirigami, as KDE Plasma's apps are (`-kde`). The GTK ones need glibc 2.39 or later (Ubuntu 24.04 and newer), the KDE ones glibc 2.41 or later (Debian 13, Fedora 42, Ubuntu 25.04 and newer).
  - AppImages bring their toolkit. Make one executable (`chmod +x`) and run it.
  - The `.tar.gz` files are the bare binaries, for a system with the toolkit installed: GTK 4.10 and libadwaita 1.4 or later, or Qt 6.10, Kirigami 6.14 and KDE's desktop style (`qqc2-desktop-style`) or later. Unpack one and run it.
